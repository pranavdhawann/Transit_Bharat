## 1. Data honesty

### What is real

Stop coordinates, ordered stop sequences and metro line geometry come from
official agency data:

| Feed | Vintage | What we use | Where it lands |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DTC/DIMTS bus (official Delhi OTD feed) | Feb 2023 snapshot | 6,342 stops / 2,964 routes / 78,515 trips / 3.28M stop-times validated; 4 representative corridors extracted as true stop-sequence segments | `data/raw/` (local only, never redistributed) → `src/data/generated/bus-corridors.json` |
| DMRC metro (GTFS-style static) | community mirror, verified against published counts | All lines: Yellow, Blue, Pink, Violet, Magenta, Red, Green and others | `src/data/generated/metro-lines.json` |

Pipeline: `node scripts/ingest-gtfs.mjs` streams each feed once, validates
columns, coordinate bounds and calendar ranges, and writes the generated
subsets plus `data/gtfs-validation.json` (a machine-readable validation report).

**Acquisition note.** `otd.delhi.gov.in` requires a terms-acceptance form per
download and was returning 503 at build time. The bus feed came from
MobilityData's mirror of the same feed (`mdb-1262`, Transitland `f-delhi~bus`,
SHA-identical twin of `mdb-3139`). Raw feeds are **not redistributed** here,
per Delhi OTD terms — only our heavily reduced derivative subsets.

**The bus snapshot is historical.** Its services end 2024-01-01. We use its
*network geometry* — stops, sequences, corridors — and never treat it as a
current timetable.

### What is synthetic

Everything below is generated by this prototype and labelled `DEMO` in the UI:

1. **All vehicle movement.** `src/lib/vehicles.ts` is a deterministic function
   of server time, interpolated along real corridors. No live government or
   operator feed is connected — unapproved integrations are against the
   hackathon rules.
2. **All delays.** The disruption is a scripted state machine, deterministic
   so a recorded demo cannot fail.
3. **Durations.** Effective speeds per mode, bus dwell ~36 s/stop, a
   central-Delhi traffic factor (0.8× north of lat 28.60), board waits
   2.5 min bus / 3.5 min metro. Deterministic estimation, not prediction.
4. **Fares.** Flat ₹20 bus; metro distance slabs ₹10–₹60. Auto-rickshaw
   ₹30 base / 1.5 km + ₹11/km at ~20 km/h simulated. Estimates, not quotes.
5. **Transit legs are emitted `DEMO`**, never as authoritative `SCHEDULED`
   times, because no live timetable backs them.

### Where this is disclosed

Provenance badge on every view · the Honesty section of `/about` · the app
footer · this file · the README.

## 2. Scope cuts (deliberate)

- **Delhi only.** No other cities.
- **Curated network.** 4 bus corridors + metro lines out of the feed's 2,964
  routes (~329 graph nodes). The ingestion pipeline handles the full feed; the
  subset keeps the graph fast and the demo judgeable.
- **Some areas are auto-only.** Laxmi Nagar, Qutub Minar and Preet Vihar are
  not in the generated metro subset, so trips there fall to the auto-rickshaw
  option. That is correct behaviour for this dataset, not a routing failure.
- **No OpenTripPlanner yet.** Routing runs on the built-in deterministic
  engine. `/api/journeys` is normalized specifically so an OTP adapter can
  replace it without UI changes. (OTP 2.x is GraphQL-only; a city graph needs
  ~4–8 GB heap.)
- **No accounts, payments, ticketing, UPI, NCMC, SOS, complaints,
  crowdsourcing, occupancy, carbon or gamification.**
- **"Arrive by" is parsed, not honoured.** The planner is "leave now" only;
  the AI parser may note a deadline but the router does not yet plan backwards
  from it. The UI says so.
- **Geolocation is opt-in and session-scoped.** The browser position is used
  only after the rider taps **Use current location**. Exact coordinates stay in
  `sessionStorage` and the journey POST body; the URL contains only a
  `current-location` marker. Consequently, a current-location journey link is
  not portable to another tab or device, and accuracy depends on the browser
  and device location services.
- **Hindi is partial.** Key journey strings, search aliases and the disruption
  explanation are bilingual. The full interface is not.

## 3. Engineering limitations

- **Walking distance drives coverage.** The router walks up to 900 m to reach
  the network. Landmarks further out get an auto first/last mile instead.
- **31 of 81 landmark coordinates are hand-entered** approximate area
  centroids. 38 were snapped to or verified against real DMRC station
  coordinates; the rest had no station to check against and may be off by a
  few hundred metres.
- **Vehicle simulator granularity.** Ping-pong interpolation along
  stop-to-stop straight lines, not road shapes. Plausible, not road-accurate.
- **Router optimality.** Dijkstra over a curated subset with composite cost
  profiles. Good on the pilot network; not a general-purpose Indian-city router.
- **Journey IDs embed a one-minute time bucket**, so deep links re-plan once
  the bucket rolls over.
- **GO-mode "Switch route"** restarts guidance from the new itinerary's
  beginning rather than rebalancing mid-junction.
- **Map tiles depend on OpenFreeMap** (positron fallback, then an offline
  notice). No SLA. The text timeline stays usable by design.
- **The disruption scenario lives in the browser** (sessionStorage), because
  serverless instances do not share memory. It dies with the tab, and if
  storage is blocked it only holds within a single page view.

## 4. AI boundary

Two features use an OpenAI model when `OPENAI_API_KEY` is set, and both are
constrained by the same rule: **the model handles language, the deterministic
planner owns every fact.**

- `/api/ai/preferences` extracts constraints from plain language (origin text,
  destination text, walking preference, deadline, interchange cap, stated
  access need). It cannot plan or alter a route.
- `/api/ai/disruption-note` phrases an explanation from already-computed
  numbers. Every digit in its output is checked against the figures we
  supplied; one unrecognised number and the whole response is discarded in
  favour of a deterministic bilingual template.

Without a key both fall back — and **say why**, on screen, with a specific
reason (`no_api_key`, `timeout`, `http_error`, …). A silent fallback would be
worse than no AI at all, because a broken key would look exactly like success.

## 5. Accessibility status

**Implemented.** Large touch targets, visible focus states, semantic
landmarks, ARIA combobox/listbox search, no colour-only status meaning (badges
always carry text), `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, keyboard shortcuts
documented on-screen in GO mode, EN/HI toggle on every page.

**Not done.** Full screen-reader journey audit; complete Hindi localisation.

Note that stated access needs (wheelchair, heavy luggage, small child, senior)
are turned into *actual routing constraints* — a walking limit and an
interchange cap the router enforces — rather than being recorded and ignored.
